    Why fibreglass suits a flat roof.

    Seamless

    Laid as one continuous layer, with no joints or seams for water to find.

    Tough finish

    Repairs we take on

    No job too small to do well.

    01

    Leaks

    Tracing where the water is getting in, often at the chimney, valleys or flat roof edges, and fixing the cause.

    02

    Slipped tiles and slates

    Refixing or replacing loose and missing tiles and slates before the weather gets underneath.

    03

    Storm damage

    Wind-lifted ridges, broken tiles and damaged lead after a storm.

    Signs to watch for

    When to call a roofer.

    General signs that a roof needs a look. If in doubt, ring.

    Damp patches on a ceiling

    Stains or damp upstairs, especially after rain, usually mean water is getting in somewhere above.

    Tiles or slates on the ground

    Pieces in the garden or on the path mean something has slipped or broken on the roof.

    Gaps around the chimney

    Lifted or cracked lead where the stack meets the roof is a common source of leaks.

    Overflowing gutters

    Water spilling over in rain points to blocked, sagging or failed gutters.

    Loose ridge tiles

    Cracked mortar or moving ridge tiles can be refixed, or replaced with a dry ridge system.
